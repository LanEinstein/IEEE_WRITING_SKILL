# Revision: how to polish without breaking the science

Polishing preserves the claim and improves how it reads. It is not rewriting for its own sake, and it is not a synonym sweep.

## RV1. Polish, do not rewrite, unless structure is the complaint

When the author asks for polish, check language and logic against the rules and change what fails. Do not produce a new paragraph with a new order of argument unless the author asked for a restructure. When the author's complaint is structural ("this section reads like a lab notebook"), a local edit per sentence cannot answer it: propose a structural plan first.

## RV2. Preservation list

Every revision keeps: object, operation, comparator, metric, direction, magnitude, quantifier, conditions, the evaluation protocol, and the strength of the evidence. Read the source table, definition or record before touching a sentence that interprets them. A wording change must never strengthen or weaken a claim.

## RV3. Scope of an edit is the author's, not the tool's

- An authorized section or subsection is completed in one pass; do not request a go for each paragraph.
- A proposal request means show the exact wording before applying anything, then wait.
- Apply only after an explicit approval word. "Pass", "fine", "ok" without scope is confirmed before applying.
- Approval of one item does not authorize neighbours. Apply exactly what was approved, and report anything else found.

## RV4. One unit per proposal

A proposal contains: the exact original, the exact replacement, and one sentence of reason. Several paragraphs are proposed as separate items with their own approval, so that partial approval is possible.

For a long or structural change, show the complete proposed paragraph, not a diff of fragments, and state which neighbours are affected. For a short sentence-level fix, the diff is enough.

## RV5. Length

Polishing does not grow the text unless the author allows it. Default: equal or shorter. When clarity genuinely needs words, say so and name what was cut elsewhere to pay for them. When a draft gains a line, recover it inside the same paragraph by removing words that carry no information before touching meaning.

## RV6. Do not re-introduce what was deleted

An approved deletion is a decision. Do not restore a removed sentence, term, number or example in a later round because the new draft reads better with it. Track removed items.

## RV7. Approved text stays revisable

Approval fixes what enters the manuscript, not what may never be improved. When a later round shows that approved text violates a rule, or reads worse than an alternative, propose the change and say that it revises approved text. Manuscript quality outranks the approval history.

## RV8. Check order

Do not start at the sentence level. Run the levels in this order, because a sentence fix inside a wrong paragraph is wasted:

1. **Section level:** does each section do its job, in the right order, without promising something it never delivers?
2. **Paragraph level:** one argument, governing first sentence, analysis after each observation, ending on theme, no repetition of neighbours.
3. **Sentence level:** information order, true connectives, one object per sentence, length, insertion, repetition.
4. **Word level:** lexicon, register, collocation, field terminology.
5. **Reference level:** referents, abbreviations, symbols defined before use.
6. **Numbers:** every value traced to its source, deltas computed by tool.
7. **Layout:** measured after the text is final, never estimated.

Report the levels that were checked; do not claim a level that was not run.

## RV9. Self-review before the author sees it

Before delivering, read the unit once as its author, once as a first-time reader, and once as a reviewer looking for a reason to reject. Fix what you find, and say in the note what the self-review changed. The author should not be the one who finds a problem the writer could have found.

A second pass by a different agent or model is useful only when it is actually available; a serial self-review is never described as independent review.

## RV10. Keep the meaning of numbers through rephrasing

If a sentence reports a comparison, keep the comparator explicit after the rewrite. "It improves every architecture" loses the reference; "P raises UF1 in each of the four tested architectures" does not. Never convert a measured difference into a qualitative adverb without saying so.

## RV11. What a polish is allowed to fix, and what it must report

Allowed without discussion: grammar, agreement, article use, punctuation within the rules, word order inside a sentence, an unclear pronoun, a false connective, a repeated word, a filler adjective.

Must be reported, not silently changed: a claim that the evidence does not support, a number that disagrees with the table, a term used inconsistently, a statement that contradicts another section, an inference that skips a step.

## RV12. When the author's instruction conflicts with a rule

The author's instruction wins inside the rules of evidence. State the conflict in one sentence, apply the instruction, and record the exception with its scope so later rounds do not re-apply the rule against it.
