# Evidence-based review rubric

Assess novelty/positioning, technical soundness, protocol/statistical reporting, evidence sufficiency for the actual claim, literature support, presentation, and cross-section claim consistency. A scoped task need not score unrelated dimensions. Scores are optional ordinal summaries, not acceptance probabilities; evidence drives the conclusion.

| Severity | Meaning | Required support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CRITICAL | A demonstrated error invalidates a central claim, or a verified hard submission requirement is violated | Source quote/location, actual contradiction or requirement, consequence |
| MAJOR | A material ambiguity, unsupported inference, or structural defect needs substantive revision | Evidence and the smallest sufficient fix; a narrower claim may resolve it |
| MINOR | Local wording/notation/caption/reference/format problem | Location and concrete repair |
| ADVISORY | Optional preference or unverified field norm | State its status; do not count it as a demonstrated defect |

Do not automatically declare a finding because a supplementary test, diagnostic, favored venue tier, or historical blueprint slot is absent. Judge whether the actual claim has sufficient support. No detected difference is not equivalence. A matched metadata record is not checked content. Unknown page caps are not violations. Language severity follows its effect on understanding, not banned-word counts.

Every finding: location/quote → evidence → consequence → minimum fix → confidence. Include counterevidence to a finding and withdraw it if facts refute it, without concession quotas. A decision estimate, when requested, is explicitly an internal qualitative assessment and its unresolved scope is stated.
