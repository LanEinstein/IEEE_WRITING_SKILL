# Paragraph card

Filled before drafting a paragraph, in English keywords. It is the buffer between a plan or a Chinese draft and the English prose; drafting from the card is what prevents translation-shaped sentences.

```text
Role in the section:   <what this paragraph must establish>
Claim (one sentence):  <the topic sentence's content>
Evidence:             <table/figure/record, with the value that carries the claim>
Mechanism / reason:   <why the evidence comes out this way, at the level the method supports>
Implication:          <what follows; the paragraph's closing point>
Link from previous:   <the relation and the visible marker>
What must NOT appear: <neighbours' facts, later-section content, internal terms>
Length budget:        <lines or words, from the section budget>
```

Checks before writing:

1. The claim is a claim, not a topic ("results are discussed").
2. The evidence exists and is traceable; if not, the claim narrows.
3. The mechanism is not a guess: it states an operation the method definition supports.
4. The link from the previous paragraph is a real relation.
5. The closing implication belongs to this paragraph's theme.

After writing, compare the paragraph with the card: a paragraph that drifted from its claim is rewritten from the card, not patched.
