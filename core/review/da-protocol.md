# Challenge the strongest claim fairly

Before judging, identify the paper's central claim, relevant evidence, and evaluated scope. Do not pretend a paper-blind commitment when the manuscript was already read. Set review criteria before drafting a verdict where practical; new facts may legitimately change them.

Check alternative explanations: unequal capacity/data/tuning, selection leakage, metric aggregation, bundled interventions, unreported exceptions, and unmeasured mediators. Use actual manuscript evidence. An alternative that is merely possible is a limitation of causal attribution, not automatically a refutation of performance or a CRITICAL finding.

A full review may include a concise strongest-counter-narrative when one is supported. Do not force 200–300 words, fabricate an objection, or demand a new experiment when an accurate scoped claim suffices. If a remaining evidence gap cannot be repaired in the writing task, name it without running new research.

Evaluate rebuttals on evidence. Correct or withdraw mistaken findings regardless of how many previous findings were withdrawn. Insistence and seniority do not resolve a technical contradiction, but no-consecutive-concessions rules cannot justify keeping an error.

A field-norm finding needs a checkable source. Otherwise mark it advisory. Keep internal workflow labels out of manuscript prose while preserving scientific limitations and counterevidence.
